# Software Engineering Skills, Jobs and Careers in the AI Era

## Overview

This Thoughtworks article examines how AI is reshaping software engineering roles, skills requirements, and career
development. The authors argue that organizations must address talent and skills implications now to avoid future
knowledge gaps and succession planning challenges.

## Key Arguments

### 1. The Future of Software Engineering Work

**From Building to Orchestrating:**

- AI can now generate complex code in seconds, shifting developer roles from code-writing to code review, testing, and
  system orchestration
- Different project types will require different AI integration approaches:
    - **Greenfield projects**: Developers move toward product owner/business analyst roles, creating technical
      specifications for AI systems
    - **Evolving AI-generated code**: Requires greater developer oversight and system understanding
    - **Code migrations**: AI can translate between languages; developers manage the translation process
    - **Legacy modernization**: Developers use AI to understand codebases, then collaborate to forward-engineer
      solutions

**The "Expert Generalist" Model:**

- The industry is seeing a pendulum swing back toward generalism, enabled by AI augmentation
- Expert Generalists possess:
    - Core programming concepts and patterns knowledge
    - Ability to decompose complex work into testable pieces
    - Collaboration skills with both programmers and stakeholders
- Example: A backend expert can now be proficient in frontend development using AI tools

### 2. Managing Attitudes to Change

- Different engineers will respond differently to role evolution
- The message isn't "adapt or die" but rather realigning abilities, interests, and passions with new work forms
- Leaders must facilitate open discussions about how developers can adapt

## Critical Challenges for Talent Development

### The Foundational Knowledge Gap

- Younger developers ("AI native" generation) use AI tools more than other cohorts
- Risk: Over-reliance on AI may cause skill atrophy on a generational scale
- Concern raised by developer Namanyay Goel: junior developers may lack foundational knowledge from struggling through
  problems
- **Critical need**: Ensure future developers learn fundamentals that help them navigate systems and handle AI-generated
  code confidently

### Building Skepticism and Understanding

- Research shows people who understand how AI systems work are more skeptical about them
- Teaching healthy skepticism is important for effective AI use alongside other skills

## Recommended Solutions

### 1. Collaborative Learning

- Mentorship and support mechanisms are essential
- Regular pairing sessions help junior developers deepen understanding
- Reading and interpreting code together is more powerful than output alone
- Experienced Expert Generalists should focus on developing future individuals and teams
- Cultivate curiosity, communication, and stakeholder management skills

### 2. Apprenticeship Model

- Formal apprenticeship programs with clear learning goals and milestones could be particularly valuable
- Benefits: Structured learning of fundamentals and how AI fits into workflows
- Challenges: Software engineering culture of self-teaching and rapid pace of change
- Potential addition: Formal accreditation/qualifications (similar to accounting, medicine, plumbing)
- **Rationale**: AI is creating accountability and authority questions that may drive professionalization

### 3. Organizational Culture and Leadership

- Create cultural cohesion where developers support one another through knowledge sharing
- Implement pathways for career cultivation at all levels
- Send clear messages that mitigate fears while encouraging reflexivity
- Leverage diverse perspectives and backgrounds
- Balance encouragement with thoughtfulness about AI's role

## Core Insight

The article emphasizes that **professionalization is often driven by crises of authority and accountability—and AI is
creating exactly that in software engineering today.** The industry must respond proactively to avoid talent decay and
knowledge gaps.

## Bottom Line

Success requires leaders (both technical and business) to actively shape how the next generation learns software
engineering fundamentals while adapting to AI-augmented workflows. The stakes extend beyond organizational success to
the health of the entire software engineering discipline.
